Plain Language under DIN 8581-1

Complex information can be easier to understand without losing important detail. I revise existing texts in Plain Language or develop a new version from your materials.

When Plain Language is the right fit

Plain Language starts with the intended readers: can they find, understand and use the information they need? DIN ISO 24495-1 describes the general principles. DIN 8581-1 applies them to German factual texts. Read what the standards cover and where their limits are.

This service may be a good fit for:

  • Customer information, help content and frequently asked questions
  • Forms, letters and administrative texts
  • Information in health and social services
  • Internal policies, handbooks and training materials
  • Website copy and digital service information

What you can commission

  • Revision of existing texts using the principles of Plain Language
  • New copy based on a brief, your notes or subject-matter materials
  • Quality assurance for internally written or AI-generated drafts
  • Consistency checks across related texts or documents

You receive a complete text, with focused questions where the source is ambiguous or a subject-matter decision is still needed.

Easy Language or Plain Language?

Easy Language and Plain Language are not interchangeable. The German Federal Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs describes Easy Language as having been developed specifically for people with disabilities, including people with learning difficulties. It uses a greater degree of simplification. DIN 8581-1, by contrast, is not intended for readers with cognitive impairments, and the rules of Easy Language are not part of that standard.

Easy Language is the main focus of my services. Plain Language complements it for projects where information should be easier to access without being simplified to the same extent. Learn more about Easy Language.
